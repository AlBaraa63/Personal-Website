Problem Statement
The High Cost
of Attrition

Replacing a single employee costs between 33% and 200% of their annual salary — covering recruitment, onboarding, and lost productivity during the transition.

Reactive HR costs millions.
Proactive data mining saves them.

"By applying data mining to historical records, predictive algorithms can identify at-risk employees before they reach the tipping point — allowing management to intervene with targeted support today."

Research Questions
Three Guiding
Questions
  • RQ1: Which features best predict attrition, and can automated selection tools isolate a meaningful subset?
  • RQ2: Which data mining algorithm delivers the strongest classification performance, and how do different algorithm families compare?
  • RQ3: What patterns and association rules can be extracted to guide HR policy and targeted retention strategies?

Core Hypothesis

Model interpretability and sensitivity to the minority class is more valuable than optimising for raw accuracy alone.

Unsupervised Learning — Clustering
Searching for
Natural Groups

Clustering was applied without the Attrition label, then clusters were mapped back to check if any group had a distinct attrition rate.

  • k-Means k=3: Converged in 3 iterations, WCSSE=128,533. Clusters of 42%/31%/27% — separated by Overtime and Job_Role, but attrition rates uniform across all groups (≈20%).
  • k-Means k=5: Converged in 3 iterations, WCSSE=123,309. Five clusters of 24%/25%/19%/15%/18% — still no cluster with a distinct attrition rate.
  • EM (numClusters=−1 & fixed k=3): Auto mode selected k=1 — all 10,000 in one cluster. Fixed k=3 gave 50%/29%/21% split, log-likelihood −25.87.

"EM selecting k=1 in auto mode is the strongest possible signal — the algorithm found no meaningful sub-group structure whatsoever. Every employee looks the same to the model."

Association Rule Mining — Apriori
Association Rules
& HR Policy

Apriori discovers "if X then Y" patterns. Due to the 80% majority class, every high-confidence rule naturally predicts that employees will stay.

  • Run 1 (conf=0.60, support=0.35): 13 cycles performed. 20 single-attribute + 10 two-attribute itemsets — all top-10 rules predict Attrition=No. Top rule: Performance_Rating=Low → No (conf=0.81).
  • Run 2 (conf=0.80, support=0.25): 15 cycles performed. 58 single + 64 two-attribute itemsets — top rule: Marital_Status=Married → No (conf=0.81). Still zero Yes-predicting rules.
  • All rules simply reflect the 80% base rate — not genuine retention signals.

"Our goal is to identify who might leave, but no actionable rules pointing to 'Yes' were found — a direct consequence of the class imbalance."

Conclusions & Recommendations
Key Findings &
Recommendations
  • Minority-class sensitivity matters more than raw accuracy — Kappa is the decisive metric, not the 80% figure.
  • J48's interpretable tree is the most HR-deployable model — stakeholders can follow its logic and justify decisions.
  • The 1,023 False Positives are valuable: "silent stayers" who are disengaged but haven't acted yet deserve proactive engagement.
  • Shift from reactive exit interviews to proactive, data-driven wellbeing check-ins triggered by score thresholds.

"Choosing a model based on its interpretability and sensitivity to the minority class is more valuable than optimising for raw accuracy alone."
